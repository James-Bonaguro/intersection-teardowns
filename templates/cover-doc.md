## 01 · What a teardown is

**Everything a prospect can see, read the way a prospect reads it.**

A teardown is an outside-in review of how a business earns customers. I look at everything a prospect can see before they ever talk to you: your website, your reviews, your listings, your events, your content, and your competitors. Then I show you what is working, where you are exposed, and the moves that matter most.

Everything comes from public sources. No form submissions, no mystery shopping, no contacting your staff. What I find is what your prospect finds, and that is the point.

## 02 · Why it is valuable

**Owners see the homepage they built. Prospects see the search result they click.**

Most owners never see their business the way a first-time prospect sees it. A teardown closes that gap in one document.

It answers three questions. What is already working, so you protect it. Where you are exposed, stated plainly and in order of importance. What to do next, narrowed to a short list of moves with the single biggest opening named first. It also says what I could not see, because an honest boundary is more useful than a confident guess.

The result is a document you can act on Monday morning. No 40-page deck, no vague recommendations, no jargon.

## 04 · How to read the report

**Every teardown follows the same structure.**

1. **The verdict.** The bottom line, a six-row scorecard, and the three things to know.
2. **What's working.** The strengths worth protecting, with hard numbers.
3. **Where you're exposed.** Online proof, social and email (graded against the social standard), and the first step a prospect takes, most important first.
4. **The biggest opening.** Today versus after the moves.
5. **The moves, ranked.** Three or four moves in order, each with how to measure it, plus a "this week" checklist. Start with move 1.
6. **Limits.** What I could not see, labeled honestly instead of filled in with guesses. A numbered sources appendix follows.

Every important claim carries its source and the date I checked it. Where I am interpreting rather than observing, I say so.
