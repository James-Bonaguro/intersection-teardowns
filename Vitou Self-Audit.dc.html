02 / WHAT'S WORKING

The seminar room is a real, repeatable engine.

Your public Eventbrite history shows 27 events and 765 attendees over about four years, in Elyria, Westlake, and Independence.5 That's about 28 people per room and roughly one event every seven to eight weeks. Small rooms in community college buildings create quiet trust, and registration captures contact details for follow-up.

The family story does the reassurance work: three generations, a founder with more than three decades in the business, and two CFP® professionals in Dan and you.2 For a cautious pre-retiree, that continuity feels personal and safe, which is exactly what a regulated financial sale needs.

Your legitimacy signals are in place: a BBB A+ rating, a BrokerCheck badge in the site footer, and an SEC-registered advisory framework through AE Wealth Management.1,6,9 All of it reads as calm and compliant to someone doing their homework.

And you post more than you probably get credit for. The Facebook page carries roughly weekly educational posts.10 The gap isn't effort.

05 / SOCIAL IS THE FRONT DOOR

Attention is the job now. Social is where authority gets built.

Social media is how most people find their way to a website today, and investors vet advisers there before they call. The firm that shows up every week, with a point of view, becomes the name people already trust when they’re ready.

~50%of investors say social media affects which adviser they hire (Hartford Funds, 2021)17
20%said an adviser’s social presence was the deciding factor (same study)17
85%of advisers using social for business use LinkedIn, the top channel (Putnam, 2020)18

NEVER RUN OUT OF TOPICS

You’re the experts on the advice. A simple scraping setup (Firecrawl or similar, feeding a spreadsheet) can pull what retirees are asking each month in forums, search, and the news, and rank it by how often it comes up. You pick, you write the take, AI helps draft, compliance approves.

SO WHAT

One post, three channels, two to three times a week, on a schedule. That’s how the seminar room’s trust reaches people who haven’t walked in yet.

07 / THE BIGGEST OPENING

Show the work before you ask for contact details.

There's a gap between the trust you earn face to face and the proof a prospect finds online. Someone who searches first finds almost no current reviews, no client stories, and no concrete picture of what a retirement-income plan actually answers for them.

Almost none of this is unique to Vitou. The gated checklist, the fitness-process branding, and the contact-form scheduling are the standard playbook across the Advisors Excel network. You aren't behind your peers. But it means the opening is wide, and whoever moves first locally leapfrogs the field.

One nearby firm is already moving: Wealth Enhancement's Rocky River page publishes local FAQs and describes what its planning delivers.11 Generic retirement articles won't stand out. Concrete, local, and specific will.

09 / BEYOND MARKETING

The first technology win is usually the admin, not the planning software.

You mentioned the team runs on Excel and Income Solver. I can't see your day-to-day from the outside, so I won't guess at your workflows. But in firms your size, with three advisers and a client-services lead, the first win is rarely the planning software. It's the repetitive work around it: turning every client meeting into clean, structured notes, keeping follow-ups from slipping, and making sure nothing a client said gets lost between the meeting and the file.

If it's useful, the first project I'd scope is meeting notes: every conversation captured, organized, and filed the same way, with a person checking the result. One workflow, measured against how you do it today. No rip-and-replace of anything that already works.
